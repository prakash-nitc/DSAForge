## The twelve

```rules
 1. Blank page before notes. Always. Every session, every template.
 2. Timer visible on every re-solve. Hit the cap, stop, log the failure.
 3. Repair patterns, not problems. One card restores 6-20 problems.
 4. Interleave after repair. Blocked practice is for learning, not readiness.
 5. Cold re-solve is the ONLY mastery signal. First solves do not count.
 6. Report L3+, never 'solved'. Today that's ~9, not 111.
 7. The mistake log is append-only and uncomfortable. Read it before every OA.
 8. Grade down freely, never up on a feeling. Levels expire in 30 days.
 9. The 39 anchors stay in rotation. Interviews or not.
10. A missed daily loop ends at the day boundary. No doubling up.
11. The skip list is binding. If it's marked x, you don't touch it in September.
12. Constraints first. n tells you the algorithm class before you know the algorithm.
```

## The five failure modes to watch for in yourself

| Mode | What it looks like | The counter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Planning-shaped avoidance | Building another tracker, resequencing the plan, asking calendar questions while a block goes unreported. | Run one card. Report the number. |
| Volume theatre | Three new easy problems to move the count while Graphs stays at zero. | Report L3+, not solved. |
| Comfort revision | Re-reading solutions, re-watching videos on solved topics. | Blank page first, always. |
| Rounding up | Logging a hinted solve as clean. | The 60-second test doesn't negotiate. |
| Breadth panic | Two days before an OA, starting a brand new topic. | §7.1 forbids it. Reactivate, don't acquire. |
